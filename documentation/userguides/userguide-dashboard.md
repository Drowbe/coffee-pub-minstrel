# Using the Dashboard

**Audience:** the Gamemaster, during a session.

How to run audio from the Dashboard: the tab you keep open while you play. Everything here is
Gamemaster only.

![The Dashboard tab: Now Playing, Music, Environment and Interface readouts above the Sound Scene, Playlist and Cue racks](../assets/product-overview.webp)

## Set up the Dashboard before a session

The Dashboard shows only what you have favourited, so it is empty until you fill it. Spend five
minutes before a game doing this and you will barely touch the other tabs during one.

1. Go to **Playlists** and click the heart on the tracks and playlists you expect to want.
2. Go to **Cues** and heart the cues you want within reach.
3. Go to **Sound Scenes** and heart the scenes for tonight's locations.
4. Come back to the Dashboard. Everything you hearted is now in the racks.

Favourite the things you will reach for *under pressure*, not everything you own. A rack with eight
scenes is faster than one with forty.

## Read what is playing

The four readouts across the top are your mixer:

- **Now Playing** names the active sound scene and its description.
- **Music**, **Environment** and **Interface** each show what is sounding on that channel, with a
  volume slider and a percentage.

Drag a channel slider to change that channel's level immediately. This is the fastest way to duck the
music for a moment of dialogue without stopping anything.

## Start and stop audio

- **Start a scene:** click the play arrow on its card in the Sound Scene Rack. The card shows
  "Playing" and the Now Playing readout changes to it.
- **Stop that scene:** click the same card's button again, or use **Stop Scene** in the bar at the
  bottom.
- **Play a single track:** click the play arrow on a row in the Playlist Rack. This does not start a
  scene; it just plays that track.
- **Fire a cue:** click its card in the Cue Rack. It plays once.

## Find something fast

Each rack has its own search box, and typing filters that rack alone as you type. Use the small
cross to clear it. Searching does not stop or change anything that is playing.

## Stop the right amount

The buttons along the bottom stop progressively more:

| Button | Stops |
|---|---|
| **Stop Scene** | The active sound scene, and every timer it was running |
| **Stop Music** | The music channel, and the scene's music sequence, so the next track will not start |
| **Stop Environment** | The environment channel, and any layer waiting to start later |
| **Stop One-Shots** | Repeating scene effects and any cue currently sounding |
| **Stop All** | Everything, and tears the scene down |

None of these are pause. Once a scene's timers are cancelled they stay cancelled until you start the
scene again.

## Recover from a mistake

**Restore**, at the bottom left, returns to the audio that was playing before the current scene
started. Use it when you have started the wrong scene: start the right one instead, or hit Restore to
get back to where you were.

**Refresh** rebuilds the window from your playlists. Use it if you have added or renamed sounds in
the Foundry sidebar while Minstrel was open and they have not appeared.

## The menubar, when the window is closed

Minstrel also puts a bar in the Blacksmith menubar showing the active scene and what is playing, with
the same stop buttons. You do not need the window open to stop audio mid-scene, which is usually what
you want when a player asks a question and you need silence now.
